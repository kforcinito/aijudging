Work stream 1

Overview

Judges spend part of every routine looking down to write comments, and that is time they are not watching the athletes. With Eyes Up, judges speak their observations hands-free. An AI assistant turns them into streamlined, refined comments that the judge reviews, edits, and copies and pastes into SimpleScore.

How it works

Capture

Hands-free voice input

Judges speak their observations out loud while they keep watching the routine. No writing, no looking down.

Refine

Coach-ready comments

The AI turns the judge's spoken observations into clean, streamlined comments, keeping the judge's own words and meaning.

Confirm

Judge review

The judge reviews and edits the refined comments, then copies and pastes them into SimpleScore. Nothing is entered until the judge approves it.

Judge stays in control: Eyes Up is an assistant, not a scorer. AI refines the wording, but every comment and score belongs to the judge.

What success looks like

  • Judges spend more of each routine watching the floor.
  • Clearer, more consistent comments for coaches.
  • Faster turnaround from the end of a routine to comments entered in SimpleScore.

Requirements: comment formatting

Functional requirements for how Eyes Up formats and categorizes comments. Every comment is either a deduction or a rule violation, written in the format judges and coaches already use, and listed in chronological order from the start of the routine.

Deductions

Video time – Deduction – Location / notes

Chronological from the beginning of the routine, using the shorthand from the deduction system and paper score sheet.

0:59 - AF - back right
1:58 - MBF - center pyramid
Violations

Routine time – Rule – Brief explanation

Same chronological format, citing the rule category and subgroup (for example, Tosses B3 or Stunts A3).

1:10 - Tosses B3 - WARNING - top person appears to perform 2 tricks during toss
2:10 - Stunts A3 - no spotter for extended stunt on right side of pyramid

Deduction shorthand

Most judges use the shorthand listed on the deduction system and paper score sheet. Each deduction comment is labeled with one of these codes.

CodeDeduction
AFAthlete Fall
MAFMajor Athlete Fall
BBBuilding Bobble
BFBuilding Fall
MBFMajor Building Fall

Violation categories

For All Star Cheer, rules are grouped into categories (General, Stunts, Pyramids, Tumbling, Dismounts, and Tosses) plus Event Rules. Each category is broken into smaller subgroups, such as B3 or A3, and each violation comment cites both.

Approved and off-limits language

Language depends on the scenario:

  • Point deductions are stated factually.
  • Rule violations may call for a warning when it can't be said factually that something happened. In the 1:10 example above, “appears” is used on purpose, together with WARNING.
  • All comments are direct, so there is no confusion when someone reads the comment and reviews the routine.

Comment guardrails

  • The AI drafts and organizes; the judge makes every final call and approves before anything is pasted into SimpleScore.
  • The AI never applies, adds, or suggests a deduction or score.
  • The AI never adds observations, severity, or advice the judge didn't say.
  • Comments match the existing customer-facing format, with no freestyling.
  • Every comment is labeled as one of the predefined deductions or violations.
