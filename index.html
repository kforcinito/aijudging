One initiative, two work streams

The AI Judging project explores how AI can make competition scoring more consistent, more transparent, and less demanding on judging panels. During the pilots, AI does not replace any judge; trained human judges keep the final call. The work is split into two complementary streams so each can be scoped, tested, and evaluated independently. Each stream has its own page with the full plan, status, and open questions.

Work stream 1

Eyes Up Judging

Keeps judges' eyes on the performance, not on the score sheet. Judges currently spend part of every routine looking down to write comments. With Eyes Up, judges speak their observations hands-free. An AI assistant turns them into streamlined, refined comments that the judge copies and pastes into SimpleScore.

  • Hands-free voice capture of judge observations
  • Clean, coach-ready comments refined from the judge's own words
  • Judge reviews and edits before pasting into SimpleScore

Role of AI: assistant only. AI refines the wording, but every comment and score belongs to the judge.

Work stream 2

AI Scoring

The AI will score routines from one division at the 2026 Youth Summit, but not live at the event due to infrastructure constraints. Instead, the AI scores 30–50 recorded routines from that division after the event, and we compare its scores to the panel's actual scores. This tells us whether the AI is accurate, consistent from run to run, and catching anything judges miss.

  • Post-event scoring of 30–50 recorded routines from one division
  • Side-by-side comparison with the panel's actual scores
  • Checks for accuracy, run-to-run consistency, and anything judges miss
  • To start: the division's rubric and a confirmed video source

AI Scoring MVP: ready Friday, April 16, 2027. See the full timeline on the stream page.

Guardrails

These principles apply to both work streams during the pilots.

Humans decide

Trained judges remain the final authority on every official score. AI supports and informs; it does not overrule.

One rubric

AI tools work from the same official scoring rubric and rules as the panel, so results are directly comparable.

Explainable

Every AI-generated score or suggestion must be traceable to specific moments in the routine and specific rubric criteria.

Athlete privacy

Video and performance data are used only for approved project purposes, stored securely, and handled with extra care for minors.

AI assists the panel. The panel owns the score.

Future direction

Today's pilots keep every judge on the panel. Over the next three years, as AI earns trust, we see its role growing to support smaller, more efficient judging panels and lighter support staffing at events.

Scale AI judging and scoring

Extend proven AI judging and scoring tools beyond the pilot to more events, divisions, and levels.

Objective rules for skills

Use AI to support clear, objective rules for skills so they are scored the same way at every event.

Cost savings

Within three years, run events with smaller, more efficient judging panels and fewer judging support staff, with AI taking on more of the work.

Earned trust: Any expanded role for AI scoring depends on evidence from AI Scoring, judge buy-in, and an explicit decision by the project leadership.
