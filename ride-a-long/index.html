Work stream 2

Overview

Ride A Long puts an AI judge on the panel as a silent observer. It watches the same routines and produces its own rubric-based scores, which are recorded privately and compared with the official human scores after the event. The aim is to learn where AI agrees with experienced judges, where it does not, and why.

How it works

Observe

Shadow scoring

Routine video from approved events is scored by the AI against the same rubric the panel uses. Its scores are never shown to judges, coaches, or athletes during the event.

Compare

Agreement analysis

After each event, AI scores are lined up against panel scores by category to measure agreement, spot systematic bias, and surface routines where the two diverge most.

Calibrate

Expert review

Senior judges review the largest gaps. Their findings refine the model and can also highlight rubric language or panel calibration that needs attention.

Shadow only: Ride A Long scores have no effect on results, rankings, or awards. They exist only to evaluate the technology.

What success looks like

  • Agreement with panel scores within an accepted tolerance across rubric categories.
  • No consistent bias by division, level, team size, or venue setup.
  • Clear explanations for each AI score that a senior judge can verify.
  • A body of evidence large enough to support a confident go or no-go decision.
